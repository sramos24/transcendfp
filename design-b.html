What's inside

The stuff that actually moves the needle between lessons.

One lesson a week is an hour. The other 167 hours are where pitchers are made. The app is how I stay with her for those.

Weeks, days, done

Programs are laid out week by week and day by day, so there's never a night where nobody knows what she's supposed to be working on.

Video on every drill

Each exercise comes with the demo, so she's copying the movement instead of trying to remember what it looked like on Tuesday.

Reps and sets tracked

She checks off what she finished. You get to stop asking. I get to see who's actually putting the work in before the next lesson.

Notes from me

Cues attached to the drills she specifically struggles with — stacking, whip, arm path — in the same words I use in the circle.

Built for a real week

Programs assume school, practice and tournaments. Short days when the week is packed, longer ones when there's room.

No app store, no cost to start

It installs straight from the browser to her home screen. Start free, upgrade when the Academy library opens.

Not a mockup

This is already live. Most people just don't know it exists.

The members app is running today at members.transcendfastpitch.com — real programs, real athletes, real drills. It's the most valuable thing Transcend has built, and right now it's buried behind a login link. This page exists to put it out front.

Meet Jiana

I was the pitcher who didn't believe herself.

Early on, my problem was never the mechanics or the hours. It was the doubt in the circle — the part nobody was coaching.

I played D1 at Saint Mary's, finished at Menlo College, and have spent the years since studying how the body is actually built to move. I teach internal rotation, and I haven't looked back.

But the mechanics are the easy half. The power of having someone in your corner is immeasurable — and I want my athletes to know their worth goes well past the sport they play.
